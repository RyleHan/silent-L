The Silent L?

A robot is told to pick up the cream cheese. It reaches for the alphabet soup. Did it not hear the instruction, or did it hear it and act on something else? We give two vision-language-action models the same scene with two different instructions and follow the instruction through the network: into the language model, through the action expert, out to the gripper.

Same initial state, same robot state, same flow-matching noise. Left: the scene's own instruction. Right: the other object named. The white crosshair is where a linear probe on π0.5's action expert places the goal at each policy query; the strip underneath tracks it between the two objects.

01 / INSTRUMENT

One scene, two instructions.

Everything that differs between the two forward passes is caused by language. We read the residual stream after every block with forward hooks that never modify the computation (hooked and unhooked actions agree to 0.0), and decode a goal position with linear probes that never saw these initial states.

Decoded goal at every block of π0.5 for the state above, first policy query, before any motion

Each cell is one probe reading (blue = the scene's object, amber = the other object). Under the swapped instruction the goal leaves the scene's object as it passes through the network; the outlined column is expert block 13, the preregistered readout.

02 / HEARD

Language creates a goal the image cannot.

Absolute object and robot positions are equally readable with or without language. The target's position is not: it becomes linearly readable only after the instruction is integrated, in both VLAs, and it survives into the action expert.

Held-out test R², mean of three probe seeds, 400 rollouts / 15,275 states / 4 object pairs. Shaded: what language adds over a prompt-blind control that sees the same image.

03 / OBEYED

What it hears is not what it does.

π0.5 obeys a swapped instruction anywhere from 6% to 100% of the time, depending on the scene. Before the arm moves, the instruction pulls the decoded goal toward the named object in every condition, including the ones where the robot then grabs the wrong thing. How far it is pulled does not predict whether the robot obeys.

Click a condition to load it into the player above. Exploratory analysis fixed in advance in the Stage 13 protocol.

04 / WRITABLE

Readable is not writable.

If the goal is in there, can we write it back in? Only when the intervention respects the model's own computation path. Patching the right residual at one block moves the action locally, then wrecks the rollout; replaying the whole pathway reproduces the policy exactly.

π0.5 on LIBERO-Object, same-state counterfactual prompts; a soft conceptor gate (COAST-style) gives 40% → 53% on a separate task with a confidence interval that still crosses zero.

05 / LEDGER

Thirteen gates, every outcome kept.

Each stage had its question, endpoint and stopping rule written down before it ran. Negative and inconclusive results stay in the record.
